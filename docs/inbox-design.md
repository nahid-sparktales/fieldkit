# Inbox UX review

The review covered finding and prioritizing work, ticket ownership, human takeover, approval review, replies and internal notes, assistant workflows, evidence, knowledge-gap follow-up, and desktop/mobile navigation.

## Boundaries

The staff inbox queries all retained conversations with 40 results per page. In Customer mode it groups by workspace customer ID before pagination, so one high-volume customer occupies one queue row. Type, status, assignment, and name/email/subject search filters are applied server-side; status counts reflect the other filters. Separate visitor identities are not merged based on matching names or emails. Tickets includes native portal and Zendesk tickets; Chatbot includes widget conversations. Drafts survive switching tickets, workspaces, and app sections during the current sign-in. They are not written to local storage, saved across reload/sign-out, or synced between devices. Leaving/reloading the browser with an unsent draft triggers the browser's standard warning.

The API's new `last_message` preview includes only customer, assistant, or staff messages, never private notes or system records. It retains customer/workspace access checks. Approval expiry is exposed in list metadata only to staff. Resolving a ticket does not imply customer-confirmed resolution, and changing colors does not grant approval permissions.

Motion is limited to short hover/press feedback; reduced-motion and increased-contrast preferences are supported. No animation or UI dependency was added.

## Follow-up review

Feedback has a persistent summary above the tabs and a dedicated panel showing resolution, experience, comment, source, and submission date. Queue cards flag negative feedback. Customers send native feedback once; unresolved responses reopen the conversation and expose the reply box without resuming a paused agent. Notes show staff names and timestamps, support search, and link back to the private-note composer. Internal notes preserve conversation status, revision, pending runs, and approvals. Rejecting an approval stops the action and moves the conversation to staff without sending a customer message.

## Customer organization

The queue starts grouped by customer, with ticket/chat counts, open counts, and colored attention indicators. Expand a customer to select a conversation; switch **Group by → Conversation** for individual rows. **All / Tickets / Chatbot** filters separate the two experiences. The group/type preference is remembered for the current browser session.

Selecting a customer name inside a ticket opens its **Customer** tab. The same profile is available under the separate **Customers** section: contact and verification details, all retained conversations, customer-wide private notes, internal notes from previous tickets, and reviewed account links. Profile navigation preserves in-memory inbox drafts. **Team** contains staff membership and invitations only. Existing reviewed account mappings move to the profile; authorization remains owner/admin only.
